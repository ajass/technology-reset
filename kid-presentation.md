# Kid Presentation — Draft Script

*To be delivered by Aaron and Denise together. Tone: straight with them, not a lecture.*

---

## Opening

You guys are getting older, and we've been thinking it's time to start giving you more control over your own time. We know we've been pretty strict about screen time, and we know that's been frustrating for you sometimes.

So here's what's going to change — and it's going to happen in two steps.

## Step 1: The Baseline (Two Weeks — November 13-20 and November 27-December 4)

Before we change anything about screen time, we're going to set up a clear schedule for what needs to happen every day and every week. Homework, chores, family meals, being part of the family. This is the floor. It happens no matter what. It's not something you do to earn screen time. It's just what being part of this family means.

For these two weeks, same rules as now, just clearer. We check your homework, we check your chores, we make sure things get done before screens. That means we hold onto your iPads until your stuff is done — when it's checked, you get the iPad back. On weekend mornings (Saturday and Sunday), you get screens from 8am to 10am as free time. At 10am sharp, we collect the iPads — that's when you get moving on your stuff. You can do your chores earlier than 10am if you want, but the expectation is you get moving at 10am. One thing: if Denise is in a meeting when you get home from school, don't interrupt her. Do your homework while you wait. When she's free, she'll check it and you get your iPad. The wait isn't a punishment — it's just the sequence. We're also going to track how it goes in a spreadsheet — four things: homework, chores, bedtime, and whether you stopped when we asked.

These two weeks are about getting used to the floor and seeing where you're at.

## Step 2: The 30-Day Trial (Starting December 11)

After the first baseline week, you go to your dad's for a week. When you come back on November 27, we do one more week of baseline — same rules, same structure. Then you go to your dad's again. When you come back on December 11, things change.

We're going to stop micromanaging your screen time. During your free time, you can decide how to spend it. If you want to play Roblox or Minecraft with your friends, that's your call. We're not going to be standing over you with a timer anymore. We're not going to pre-check your chores before you get on screens. You'll have your iPads — we won't be holding onto them anymore. That's on you now.

But homework is the one thing that stays parent-checked. You do it on your own time — we're not telling you when to do it. But you have to show us your work before screens. Doing it fast and sloppy doesn't count. If we check it and it's not good enough, you fix it, then screens. Same with instrument practice — actually practice, don't just get it out and say you're done.

Here's the deal: if we find you on screens and your stuff isn't done, we'll redirect you. "Get it done, then screens are back." That's not a punishment — it's just the sequence. If you comply, even if you grumble, that's a yellow on the tracker. If you refuse, that's a red.

For your birthdays and Christmas, you're going to get access to the gaming laptops. You'll have your own guest accounts on them. You can use them for gaming during your free time.

## What Stays the Same (Both Steps)

Bedtime screen rules stay the same. No recreational screens before school. YouTube stays blocked on your iPads but you can watch it on the TV.

Here are the specific daily rules, and we're putting them on the fridge so everyone can see them:

- Homework first — if you have homework, it gets done before screens. Homework means anything you're required to do at home that's educational or practice. That includes school assignments (even if they're not due for a week or more),, instrument practice if you take lessons, studying when a test is coming up, online school work like Google Classroom or IXL, and any other required educational stuff. Chores are not homework — those are separate. Reading is not homework — that happens after screens off, before lights out.
- Bedtime is 9:30pm, same as your dad's house
- Screens off 30 minutes before bedtime, so at 9:00pm — and that means ALL screens. Phones, iPads, TV, everything. Even reels. On Friday and Saturday nights, screens off at 9:30pm and bedtime is 10:00pm.
- Garbage and recycling bins go out to the garage before screens. You alternate — Sean one day, Liam the next.
- When we ask you to help with something that's not on the schedule — like holiday decorations or coming to Costco — you help. No pushback. That's part of being in this family.

These aren't changing. They're staying up on the fridge for everyone to see.

## The Weekly Chores

On weekend mornings (Saturday and Sunday), you get free time on screens from 8am to 10am. At 10am sharp, screens off — get dressed, eat, ready for the day. You can do your chores earlier than 10am if you want, but the expectation is you get moving at 10am.

On Saturdays, after 10am, you do your weekly chores before screens come back on:

- Laundry — wash, dry, and start putting away your own clothes
- Vacuum — downstairs and upstairs
- Clean bathroom

Once those are done, screens are on for the rest of the day.

On Sundays, it's just a spot check — room tidy, bed made, stuffies picked up, laundry put away. Lighter than Saturday.

## The Tracker

We have a tracker — it's a shared spreadsheet that Aaron and Denise fill in at the end of each day. Four things: did you do your homework, did you do your chores, did you make bedtime, and did you stop when we asked without pushback. Green, yellow, or red for each one. It takes 30 seconds. The data does the talking — not our gut feeling.

During the baseline weeks, the tracker shows us where you're starting from. During the 30-day trial, the tracker shows us whether the freedom is working.

## Gaming

The laptops are for Roblox and Minecraft. We know you're going to see your friends playing games on Steam, Epic Games, Blizzard, and other platforms, and you're going to want those too. We're going to look at games one at a time, not ban whole platforms. So if there's a game you want, bring it to us and we'll look at it together. Some games are rated for teens 13 and up, and some have chat features we're not comfortable with yet. We're not saying never. We're saying not right now. As you get older and show us you can handle the freedom you're getting now, we'll revisit.

## What Happens If It's Not Working

If you're on screens and you haven't done your stuff, we'll redirect you. Get it done, then screens are back. That's not a punishment — it's just the sequence.

If the tracker shows a pattern — three or more reds in a week — we'll sit down and look at the data together. The data shows this isn't working yet, and that's okay. We'll go back to a checked structure for two weeks where Aaron or Denise verifies your homework and chores before screens open. That's basically going back to Step 1 for two weeks. Then we try again.

There's always a path back to the freedom. Show us you can handle the structure, and it comes back.

## Close

We want to hear what you think. If you have ideas about the chore schedule or how you want to organize your time, we want to hear them.

Any questions?

## Practical Notes for Aaron and Denise

1. **Deliver this together.** Both present, same message, no disagreement in front of them. If you disagree, work it out beforehand.
2. **Have the chore/homework schedule drafted before this conversation,** but present it as a draft they can give input on. "Here's what we're thinking. What do you think?" Not "here are your new chores."
3. **Let them react.** They will have feelings about the Steam/Epic Games restriction. Acknowledge it ("we know that's disappointing") without relitigating it. The decision is made. But hear them out.
4. **Be clear about the two steps.** The baseline week is not the trial. The baseline week is parent-enforced — same structure as now, just clearer. The trial is when autonomy starts. If they ask "so when do we get freedom?" the answer is "December 11, after two baseline weeks and your dad's weeks."
5. **The 30-day trial start date should be concrete.** "Starting December 11" — when they're back from their dad's second week. Vague timelines invite testing.
6. **Write the responsibility structure down.** Put it on the fridge or somewhere visible. Not as a threat, as a reference. "What do I have to do today?" → point to the list.
7. **Show them the tracker.** They should know the data exists and what's being tracked. Transparency builds trust — they know we're not just going by gut feeling.
8. **Explain the rollback in terms of the two steps.** "If the data shows it's not working, we go back to Step 1 for two weeks, then try Step 2 again." This makes it feel like a process, not a punishment.
