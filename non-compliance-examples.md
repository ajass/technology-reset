# Non-Compliance Examples — Yellow and Red Marks

*What earns a Yellow or Red on the tracker, with concrete examples.*

---

## How Scoring Works

Each day, Aaron or Denise marks Sean and Liam separately on four categories: **Homework**, **Chores**, **Bedtime**, and **Screen Transition**. Each gets Green, Yellow, or Red.

The scores are about **behavior, not mood.** If they comply but grumble, that's still Yellow (complied). If they refuse, that's Red.

---

## Phase Context

**Phase 1 — Baseline (Nov 13-20):** Parents pre-check the floor before screens open. If the floor isn't met, screens don't come on — the parent holds the gate. Yellow/Red marks in Phase 1 are about how they responded to the parent's check: did they do it when asked, or did they push back / refuse? Phase 1 data establishes the baseline — what their behavior looks like under parent-enforced structure.

**Phase 2 — Trial (Nov 27 - Dec 27):** Boys self-manage. There is no pre-check gate. Yellow/Red marks in Phase 2 are about self-management failures that the parent caught: they were on screens with the floor unmet, parent redirected, and the mark reflects their response to the redirect. Phase 2 data tests whether autonomy works.

**Rollback (if triggered in Phase 2):** Returns to Phase 1 structure for 2 weeks. Scoring works like Phase 1 during rollback.

## The Rollback Trigger (Phase 2 only)

Rollback is not triggered by a single Red. It's triggered by a **pattern: 3+ Reds in a week** across any categories.

| Scenario | What happens |
|-----------|--------------|
| Liam has one Red on Chores (forgot garbage) and one Yellow on Screen Transition (grumbled at dinner) in a week | No rollback. Two marks, only one Red. Tracker shows it, we keep an eye on it. |
| Sean has three Reds in one week — one on Homework (didn't do reading), one on Screen Transition (refused at dinner), one on Bedtime (refused at 9pm) | /rollback Rollback conversation. "The data shows this isn't working yet." Aaron or Denise verifies homework and chores before screens open (back to Phase 1 structure). For 2 weeks. Then re-test. |
| Both boys are mostly Green and Yellow all week, no Reds | Trial continues as-is. Working. Keep going. |

---

## Key Principles

1. **One Red is data, not a crisis.** It gets logged, you move on. The pattern is what matters.
2. **Yellow is expected.** They're 11-12. Grumbling is normal. Yellow means the system is working — they complied, just not happily.
3. **Green doesn't mean perfect.** It means they did what was expected without being asked. That's the goal.
4. **The tracker is not a punishment tool.** It's a measurement tool. The data informs the conversation, not the penalty.
5. **Rollback is not grounding.** It's a structural tightening — "you showed you're not ready for this level of freedom yet, so we're going back to Phase 1 structure, and we'll try again." There's a time limit (2 weeks) and a clear path back.
6. **Phase 1 and Phase 2 scoring measure different things.** Phase 1 measures baseline behavior under parent-enforced structure. Phase 2 measures self-management under autonomy. Comparing the two shows whether autonomy improved or degraded behavior — that's the point of the 30-day review.
