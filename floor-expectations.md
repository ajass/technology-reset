## Two Phases — Read This First

The floor (homework, chores, family participation) is the same throughout. What changes is who enforces it and when.

### Phase 1 — Baseline (Nov 13-20 and Nov 27-Dec 4): Parent-Enforced

Parents pre-check each floor item before screens open. The boys do not self-manage yet. This is structured enforcement with a tracker running alongside it to collect baseline data.

- Parent checks homework: "Did you finish? Show me." → then screens
- Parent checks chores: "Is your laundry done? Did you vacuum?" → then screens
- Parent enforces all transitions (10am, 9pm screens off, bedtime)
- **iPads physically held by parent until floor is verified.** School day mornings: iPads with parent from arrival — no morning screen time. Weekend mornings (Saturday, Sunday, weekday off): screens available 8am-10am as free time; at 10am parent collects iPads. Parent hands over the iPad after the floor is checked. Parent collects the iPad at screens-off time.
- **The check happens when the parent is available, not on demand.** Denise works from home and may be in meetings when the boys get home from school. The iPads stay with her until she's free — the boys wait. This is intentional: the wait is part of the baseline data. If they can't wait without interrupting her work, that tells us about their impulse control.
- Tracker marks collected daily

> **Note for Denise — why this has to be parent-enforced for the first two weeks:**
>
> 1. **Physical gating is less work for you than parental controls.** Software means you manage unlock requests, timers, and "can you unlock it now?" pings during your meetings. Physical gating means the iPads are with you — the boys wait until you're free. No software to configure, no unlock requests to field mid-meeting.
> 2. **The wait is data.** If the boys can't wait 30-60 minutes for an iPad without interrupting your work, that tells us something about their impulse control — which is exactly what we're assessing during baseline. Nagging during meetings is a tracker item (pushback), not just an annoyance.
> 3. **The check happens when you're available, not at 4pm sharp.** Boys come home, iPads are with you, they do homework (or don't). When you're free from meetings — 4:30, 5:00, whenever — you check homework and hand over the iPads. If homework isn't done, they do it then. Less screen time while waiting is fine.
> 4. **This is temporary.** After two weeks, the iPads are theirs to manage. The physical gating is Phase 1 only.

### Phase 2 — Trial (Dec 11 - Jan 10): Autonomy with Steering

The floor is established. Boys self-manage their free time. Parents step back from pre-checking and steer on failure instead.

- Boys do homework, chores, and transitions on their own
- If on screens with floor unmet → parent redirects: "Get it done, then screens are back." Comply = Yellow. Refuse = Red.
- No pre-check gate
- 3+ Reds in a week → rollback to Phase 1 structure for 2 weeks, then re-test

### What's Different

| | Phase 1 — Baseline | Phase 2 — Trial |
|---|---|---|
| Floor check | Parent pre-checks before screens | Boy self-manages, parent steers on failure |
| Screen access | Parent grants after floor verified | Self-granted within the floor |
| Device gating | iPads physically held by parent | iPads available — self-managed |
| Parent role | Enforcer | Observer / steerer |
| If floor unmet | Screens stay closed until done | Redirect + log, screens resume after |

## What Counts as Homework

Homework is anything you're required to do at home that's educational or practice in nature. The due date doesn't matter — if it's assigned, do it before screens.

- **School assignments** — including long-term projects not due for a week or more
- **Instrument practice** — if you take music lessons, practice is homework
- **Studying for tests** — when a test is scheduled, studying is homework
- **Online school work** — Google Classroom, IXL, any digital assignments
- **Other required work** — if it's required and educational or practice in nature (religious ed, Scouts, tutoring, etc.), it's homework. Left open as new activities come up.

Not homework:
- Chores — those are separate floor items
- Reading — reading happens after screens off, before lights out. Not a screen gate.
- Optional/enrichment activities you choose to do on your own

## What Changes (the autonomy shift — Phase 1 to Phase 2)

- We stop actively timing gaming sessions during free time
- We step back from routine monitoring of conversations (safety protections stay)
- They get more say in how they spend free time, within the floor
- We shift from "enforcing screen limits" to "checking in on how things are going"

- If floor unmet while on screens: redirect (Phase 2) instead of gate (Phase 1)

## Consequences

**In the moment:**
- Phase 1: If floor isn't met, iPads stay with the parent. The devices are physically unavailable until the floor is done and verified.
- Phase 2: If on screens and the floor isn't met, we redirect. Get it done, then screens are back. Comply (even grudgingly) = Yellow on the tracker. Refuse = Red.

**Pattern (3+ Reds in a week — Phase 2 only):**
- We sit down with the tracker data. The data shows this isn't working yet.
- Structural rollback to Phase 1: Aaron or Denise verifies homework and chores before screens open. iPads physically held by parent again. For 2 weeks.
- After 2 weeks, we re-test autonomy (return to Phase 2).

**There's always a path back to autonomy.** Show us you can handle the structure, and the freedom comes back.
